Lust is a terrifying emotion. It preys on your mind, eating away at you while you enjoy the feeling or the process. It starts with a tentative invitation. It sits at the back of your mind, beckoning you to come closer. You can never shut it off. It stays there, inconspicuous but ever-present.

One day, you are tired, and suddenly it’s right in front of you. You fight a war in your mind. Normally, these wars are bound for defeat. The flesh is weak and easily tempted. So you decide to take its offer. You tell yourself: just a little bit, just a peek, just a thought, just a feel, and I’ll stop after that. But it usually doesn’t end there. It’s a path with remorse and regret waiting at the end. You know this because you’ve experienced it before, but you brush it aside each time, ignoring the warnings.

While you enjoy the process, Lust erodes your will and dulls your mind. You never notice. It does so furtively, all the while weakening your willpower. Once you have tasted the beginning, it whispers: “Go one step further, just a little bit more. We won’t take it all the way. One more step. Just one.” So you believe it. You take the extra step. Now you are two steps closer.

Throughout the process, wars are constantly fought. The struggle is agonizing. The certainty of past consequences, reminding you of your previous defeat, constantly tugs at your left, while the allure of Lust’s beauty and its climax pulls you from your right. You are torn. Devastated. Some battles are won. Some are lost. Some last for days; others, for weeks.

When your defeat is all but certain, you find yourself in a void. Regret fills your stomach, yet your mind is apathetic. Suddenly, you lose your connection with the world: the plants, the mountains, the sky, and most importantly, the people. You find yourself alone in this vast, empty abyss of darkness. You are a lost sheep without any guidance.

At this point, Lust appears again. You can choose to repeat the cycle, or you can choose to break free from it. The challenge is uphill and often takes weeks before you feel like yourself again. You won’t be restored immediately. In the process, you will gasp for existence. But I pray that you will succeed. Though you may repeat the cycle, never give up.
